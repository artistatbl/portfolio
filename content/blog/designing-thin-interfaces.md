Most products do not feel heavy because they have too many features. They feel heavy because everything arrives with the same visual weight.

When I think about a thin interface, I am usually trying to remove pressure from the screen. That means fewer loud surfaces, less competition between sections, and a clearer sense of what deserves focus first.

## What I usually look for

- Strong spacing before strong decoration
- A small number of text sizes used with intention
- Quiet borders instead of stacked cards everywhere
- One obvious action instead of several medium-priority ones

## Why this matters

A portfolio or product site should feel easy to scan on the first pass, but it should also reward a slower read. Thin design helps with both. It creates enough structure to guide the eye without making the layout feel boxed in.

That is also why I like drawer patterns for deeper content. The main page can stay calm and compressed, and the full story can still be available when someone wants to lean in.

## Writing for this kind of layout

I usually write short paragraphs, direct headings, and a summary that can stand on its own in the list view. If the list item already says enough to earn a click, the full drawer can do the quieter work of detail, context, and reflection.
